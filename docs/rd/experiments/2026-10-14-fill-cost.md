## Hurdle

To keep the GPU copy from lagging behind native scrolling, the canvas is
part of the document (C2 hypothesis 1): it covers the viewport plus one
viewport above and below, and the browser's compositor thread scrolls it
with the page. Every redraw fills all three viewports, though only one is
visible when the frame is shown. On an M1 Pro the main pass on
`perf.html` takes 4.63 ms p50 while scrolling (no opacity groups,
`2026-10-14-group-pass-cost/2026-10-14-m1pro-profile-scroll.txt`), 56% of
the 8.33 ms budget at 120 Hz, and draw culling showed the cost is fill,
not vertices (`2026-10-14-draw-culling.md`). The margins only matter for
the time between the compositor scrolling and the next redraw.

## Why existing knowledge doesn't resolve it

Browsers solve the same problem inside their compositors by rasterising
tiles around the viewport ahead of time and prioritising by scroll
direction (Chromium's tile manager), but a page script can't see when the
compositor will scroll or how far, and can't render on the compositor
thread. How large a band a script-driven canvas needs, and how often a
stalled main thread would reveal the undrawn margin, isn't documented for
this setup. The earlier measurement of the canvas's fill cost was in
software rendering only ("needs checking on real hardware", activities
C2 result 1).

## Hypothesis

Drawing only the viewport plus a band above and below it, sized to twice
the largest scroll step of the last 8 frames and at least 128 CSS px
(scissored; the rest of the canvas cleared), cuts the main pass GPU time
on `perf.html` while scrolling by at least 40% on the M1 Pro, with no
change in the visual harness (sections are captured in view) and no
change in CPU time per frame.

## Why the outcome is uncertain

- On a tiled GPU, clearing and storing the whole canvas still costs
  bandwidth, and vertex work and tile binning for the culled records run
  regardless of the scissor; the saving may be far smaller than the area
  ratio.
- The band's edge is where the compositor's scroll can reveal undrawn
  canvas: during smooth scrolling the next frame redraws it, but if the
  main thread stalls (layout, GC), blank strips may show. How large and
  how often is unknown; that is the cost side, measured below.
- Groups, backdrops and post passes read or write outside the viewport
  (blur radii, group extents); scissoring may change their pixels at the
  band edge.

## Method

1. Option `canvasFill: 'full' | 'band'` (default 'full' until decided);
   `band` scissors the main pass and skips groups outside the band, in
   `canvasPin: 'page'` only. `stats().fillRows` reports the band height.
2. `perf.html?band` and `test:profile --band` for A/B on the same build.
3. Measures: main pass GPU time (`alone` and total), CPU per frame, on the
   M1 Pro (`npm run test:profile -- --headed --only scroll` with and
   without `--band`) and in SwiftShader; visual harness with `band` as
   the default must show no new failures.
4. Stall check: stop the compositor's frames, scroll by 600 px, capture:
   the share of the viewport left blank with `full` vs `band`.

Observations so far:

- In software rendering the main pass cost follows the drawn area closely
  (43% of the rows, 40% of the time).
- `fx-blur-scissor` fails with `band`, as expected: it stops the
  compositor, scrolls half a viewport and checks the canvas below the old
  viewport, which `band` leaves cleared. It tests exactly the property
  `band` gives up.
- The stall check shows the cost side: after a 600 px scroll with no
  redraw, about half the viewport is blank with `band` (the rows past the
  128 px band), against none with `full`. CPU per frame is unchanged.

- On the M1 Pro the saving is much smaller than the area drawn: 43% of
  the rows cost 74% of the time. On a tiled GPU the vertex and binning
  work for everything on the canvas, and the clear and store of the whole
  target, run regardless of the scissor.
- The same `--nodim --full` measure read 4.63 ms in the group experiment
  earlier the same day and 5.09 ms here: run-to-run spread is ~10%, so the
  -26% is outside the noise but its exact size isn't.
- The slow tail got worse with `band` (p99 7.20 vs 6.36 ms, max 8.51 vs
  6.74 ms). Not explained; a single run each, so it may be noise.
- No late or dropped frames in either mode: at dpr 1 the full canvas
  already fits the 120 Hz budget on this machine.

## Evaluation

Not supported on the M1 Pro at dpr 1: the main pass fell 26%, short of
the 40% threshold, and its tail didn't improve. Supported in software
rendering (-60%), where the cost follows the area drawn. Against that, a
stalled main thread reveals blank canvas over about half the viewport
after a 600 px scroll (the stall check), which `full` never does.

## Conclusion and next step

Known now: on Apple's tiled GPU, the canvas margins cost about a quarter
of the main pass at dpr 1, not two thirds; most of the frame is work the
scissor doesn't remove. Trading stall robustness for that is not worth it
by default. `canvasFill` stays `'full'` by default; `'band'` stays as an
option for software rendering and as the A/B for later work.

Next, if fill becomes the bottleneck:

1. The same A/B at dpr 2 (`--dpr 2`, a Retina display's real density),
   where fill is four times larger; the share saved may differ.
2. Cut the work the scissor leaves: cull draws to the band as well as to
   the canvas (`gpu/cull.ts` takes any region), so vertex and binning work
   shrink with it.
3. If a band is adopted, keep the margins' last contents (a persistent
   scene texture, redrawn only in the band, copied to the canvas) so a
   stall shows stale content instead of blank.
