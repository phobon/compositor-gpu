## Hurdle

A one-word text edit on `perf.html` (the `mutate` scenario: a same-length
word swap or a class toggle in one card per frame) costs ~32 ms of CPU
per frame on an M1 Pro: ~11 ms in the read and ~18 ms in instance
uploads, with frames up to 58 ms (`2026-10-14-cull-index.md` evidence).
That is two to four frames at 60-120 Hz for a change confined to one of
400 cards. A partial read re-reads only the edited card, but the costs
look proportional to the page.

## Why existing knowledge doesn't resolve it

From the code, after a partial read the reader rebuilds the whole scene
(`rebuildScene`: flatten the element tree, assign paint order, sort the
layers, build batches, whose overlap test inserts every glyph rect into
a grid), and every layer is marked dirty, so each pass re-packs all its
instances (the text pass resolves each of ~70k glyphs). Which of these
dominates on real hardware isn't known; in SwiftShader (shared CPU,
not representative) a temporary breakdown put the scene rebuild at
34-123 ms per edit (batches 19-105 ms of it) and the text upload at
35-96 ms.

## Hypothesis

On the M1 Pro, rebuilding the scene and packing the text layer account
for at least 80% of `mutate`'s CPU per frame, with building batches the
largest part of the scene rebuild; the DOM reads themselves are a small
part.

## Why the outcome is uncertain

Allocation and GC differ between SwiftShader runs in the container and
Chrome on the M1 Pro; the DOM reads (style and layout queries for the
card) may cost more there than the scene work.

## Method

1. Permanent profile rows: `scene` (part of `read`: rebuilding the scene
   from the tree), `batches` (part of `scene`) and `text` (part of
   `upload`), from `reader.lastSceneMs`, `scene.lastBatchesMs` and the
   text pass's upload time.
2. `test:profile --headed --only mutate --nodim` on the M1 Pro;
   SwiftShader for the plumbing.

## Evaluation

Supported. On the M1 Pro, rebuilding the scene (10.5 ms, of which
batches 7.6 ms) and packing the text layer (16.3 ms) are 86% of an
edit's 31.3 ms; the DOM reads for the edited card are 0.6 ms. Culling
adds 1.8 ms because new batches invalidate its cache. All of it scales
with the page, not the edit.

## Conclusion and next step

Known now: a one-word edit costs what rebuilding and re-uploading the
whole page costs, because a partial read ends in a full scene rebuild
and every layer is re-packed. Next experiment: patch the scene in
place when an edit doesn't change the structure (same records, same
counts, same contexts), keep the batches when geometry is unchanged,
and upload only the changed instances.
